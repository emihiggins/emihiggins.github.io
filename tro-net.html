tro-net.com — a scam & phishing email checker built for people 60 and up.

My parents kept forwarding me suspicious emails asking "is this real?" The answer shouldn't depend on whether their daughter picks up the phone. So I designed and built tro-net end to end — product, safety engineering, billing, and the business behind it.

How it works

The problem

Modern phishing emails are well-written, well-designed, and disproportionately target older adults. The old advice — "look for spelling mistakes" — no longer works.

When someone's parent gets an email that looks like it's from their bank, they either guess, call their kids, or click. tro-net gives them a trustworthy fourth option.

The reply they get back

Forward any email to a personal address like u_1234@mail.tro-net.com and, within minutes, get back:

Safe Suspicious Likely scam

What's in the reply

  • A one-word verdictSafe, suspicious, or likely scam — rendered large and high-contrast.
  • Two or three plain-English reasonsSo users build intuition over time instead of just being told what to think.
  • Verified contact detailsIf it impersonates a real company, the real phone & website — looked up server-side from a curated allowlist, never taken from the suspicious email.

Design principles

The audience is 60+ and skeptical of unfamiliar technology — often because of the scams they've already encountered. Every product decision is filtered through that.

  • No jargon, anywhere the user can seeThe words "AI", "algorithm", "model", and any vendor name are absent from every user-facing surface. Plain language only: "we read the email and tell you."
  • Trust-first writingThe FAQ opens with "Will you read all of my emails?" and "Will you ever ask me for my password?" — privacy questions come before feature questions.
  • Big type, big tap targets, generous spacingOversized body copy, every phone number a tel: link, high contrast throughout.
  • The product will never call the user — everThat promise is baked into the code and the outreach program. Even institutional outreach is scoped to senior centers and libraries, never end users — because calling individuals would violate the exact behavior we're teaching people to distrust.

Safety invariants, enforced in code

Contact details can't leak

The classifier is structurally incapable of surfacing a phone number. It emits an organization slug (wells-fargo); a server-side resolver looks that slug up in a curated allowlist and attaches verified contact details. A runtime "belt" (stripForbiddenContact) drops any phone- or URL-shaped string from model output before it renders — and if every reason gets stripped, the system falls back to a hedged verdict telling the user to look the company up themselves.

Cached "safe" must be earned

The verdict cache only serves a safe result when the original check ran with high confidence. Below that floor, the cache is a miss and the classifier re-runs.

Hostile input by default

Forwarded content is wrapped in <untrusted_body> tags with explicit prompt-injection defenses; invisible Unicode is stripped; input is length-capped to prevent token-bill DoS. The sender allowlist is always enforcing — a leaked forwarding address is useless to a third party — and every outbound email passes a single gate enforcing RFC 3834 loop prevention, suppression lookups, and per-thread reply caps.

Architecture

The hot path

From a forwarded email to a delivered verdict — an idempotent, retryable pipeline:

  1. Inbound webhook from Postmark hands off to Inngest.
  2. Identify user, resolve thread, persist message, check quota.
  3. Pre-analysis gates run first — self-test detection, empty-body short-circuit, content-hash verdict cache, and signal extraction (auth headers, brand-mismatch, attachment fingerprinting). Easy cases resolve here; harder ones route to a more capable model.
  4. The classifier runs, with model selection driven by signal strength.
  5. The verdict is cached (only if it clears the cacheability filter), the reply is composed with proper RFC 5322 threading headers, and the outbound gate sends it.

End to end, solo: the classifier pipeline (prompt & injection defenses, verdict schema, model routing, pre-analysis gates, trusted-contacts allowlist, safety belts); the email machinery (thread resolution, RFC 5322 threading, RFC 3834 loop prevention, suppression, per-user allowlist, single outbound gate); the marketing site written for the 60+ audience; billing & accounts (Stripe subscriptions, gifting, coupons, referral credits, click-to-cancel, admin + audit log); and business ops — LLC formation, USPTO trademark filing, privacy/terms drafting, and outreach to senior centers, libraries, and Area Agencies on Aging.
